The Cannabis Equipment Financing Challenge — Federal vs. State Law

Cannabis is legal in some form in 38+ states, but it remains a Schedule I controlled substance under federal law — placing it in the same category as heroin. This creates a fundamental financing problem: virtually all banks and credit unions are federally chartered or federally insured, and lending to a federally illegal business puts their charter, FDIC insurance, and regulatory relationships at risk.

The practical result is that most conventional equipment lenders — bank-affiliated finance companies, SBA lenders, captive OEM lenders — cannot and will not finance cannabis equipment. This has created a specialized market of private credit funds, non-bank equipment leasing companies, and cannabis-focused financial services firms that operate outside the federal banking framework.

The good news for cannabis operators is that this specialized market has grown substantially since 2018. Competition among cannabis lenders has driven rates down from the 25–40% range seen in 2018–2020 to a more typical 12–20% range for established, licensed operators with clean financials in 2024. The equipment itself — particularly industrial extraction systems and commercial LED lighting — has enough commercial applications outside cannabis that some mainstream equipment lessors will finance it if the collateral can be described and valued in non-cannabis terms.

Frequently Asked Questions — Cannabis Equipment Financing

Why won't most banks finance cannabis equipment?

Cannabis remains a Schedule I controlled substance under federal law, making it illegal at the federal level regardless of state legalization status. Federally chartered banks and credit unions risk their charters, deposit insurance, and regulatory standing if they knowingly provide banking services — including loans — to cannabis businesses. Most large and regional banks refuse cannabis financing entirely. Specialized cannabis lenders, private credit funds, and equipment leasing companies not subject to federal banking charters have stepped into this gap.

What interest rates can I expect on cannabis equipment financing?

Cannabis equipment financing typically carries a 15–30% premium over conventional equipment financing rates. Where a conventional equipment loan might be priced at 7–9% for a qualified borrower, cannabis-specific equipment financing commonly runs 12–18% for established, licensed operators. Startups and operators in newer legal markets may see rates of 18–28% or higher. The premium reflects the federal legal risk borne by the lender, limited secondary market for cannabis-specific equipment, and regulatory uncertainty. Rates have been gradually declining as the legal cannabis market matures.

What cannabis extraction systems are available and what do they cost?

The main cannabis extraction technologies are CO2 extraction ($50,000–$500,000+), ethanol extraction ($15,000–$300,000), hydrocarbon (BHO/PHO) extraction ($20,000–$150,000), and rosin press mechanical extraction ($2,000–$50,000). CO2 systems from companies like Apeks Supercritical or Waters are the most expensive but produce the cleanest extract and are generally easier to finance (the equipment has industrial applications outside cannabis). Ethanol systems from Delta Separations are mid-market. Hydrocarbon systems require explosion-proof rooms, adding facility cost.

How much do commercial LED grow lights cost for a cannabis facility?

Commercial LED grow fixtures for cannabis range from $500 to $2,000 per fixture depending on wattage, spectrum, and brand. A 5,000 square foot canopy facility with fixtures spaced every 4x4 feet requires approximately 312 fixtures — at $800 average that's $250,000 in lighting alone. LED lighting can be financed as part of a complete facility buildout or as a standalone equipment loan. Many LED manufacturers (Fluence, Gavita, California Lightworks) offer financing programs through third-party lenders.

Can cannabis equipment be leased instead of purchased?

Yes. Equipment leasing for cannabis is available through specialized cannabis equipment lessors. Leasing has advantages in this industry: lower upfront capital, ability to upgrade as technology improves, and potential off-balance-sheet treatment. Some extraction equipment companies (particularly CO2 equipment suppliers) offer lease-to-own programs to bypass banking restrictions. True operating leases with FMV buyouts are available but less common — most cannabis equipment financing is structured as a capital lease or conditional sale.

What documentation do cannabis equipment lenders require?

Cannabis equipment lenders require: (1) valid state cannabis license (dispensary, cultivator, processor, or all-in-one), (2) 2 years of business tax returns or 2 years of business bank statements, (3) business financial statements or P&L, (4) personal guarantee from principal owners, (5) business formation documents, and (6) description of equipment being financed. Some lenders also require proof of compliant cash management and a copy of the state's compliance inspection report. Stronger documentation packages yield better rates.
