The Recycling Industry and Equipment Financing

The recycling and waste processing industry is one of the most commodity-sensitive manufacturing sectors. Equipment lenders who understand recycling know that a baler's value as collateral is only part of the picture — the other part is whether the recycling operator has stable revenue to service the debt. When cardboard (OCC) prices spike, recyclers thrive; when China imposed National Sword import restrictions in 2018, OCC prices crashed from $100+/ton to near zero, triggering significant financial stress across the industry.

This commodity sensitivity shapes how lenders approach recycling equipment financing. Established recyclers with long-term contracts, diverse material streams (not just one commodity), and strong balance sheets get the best terms. Startups or single-commodity operations face more scrutiny. That said, the physical equipment itself — steel-framed balers, industrial shredders, sorting conveyors — is inherently valuable and repossessable, giving lenders meaningful collateral confidence on well-maintained machinery.

Major brands in recycling equipment include Harris Equipment (Columbia, SC), Maren Engineering (South Holland, IL), Machinex (Plessisville, QC), and CP Group (San Diego, CA) — all of whom sell through dealer networks with dealer-arranged financing available. For large-scale MRF projects, manufacturer-direct financing programs or SBA 504 loans are common.

Frequently Asked Questions — Recycling Equipment Financing

How does commodity pricing affect recycling equipment financing?

Recycling equipment financing is uniquely tied to commodity markets. When scrap metal, cardboard (OCC), and plastics prices are high, lenders are more comfortable extending credit because the business cash flow is strong and the collateral (recycling equipment) has a clear revenue context. When commodity prices crash — as they did in 2018 when China restricted recyclable imports — recycling businesses face cash flow crunches and lenders tighten. Lenders who specialize in recycling equipment evaluate both the equipment value and the operator's revenue diversity and commodity contracts when pricing a loan.

What is the difference between a vertical baler and a horizontal baler?

Vertical balers ($8,000–$30,000) are smaller, lower-capacity machines suited for single-location businesses generating moderate cardboard and plastic volume — retail stores, warehouses, small distribution centers. Horizontal balers ($25,000–$200,000+) are commercial recycling plant machines that process continuous streams of material with much higher throughput. For financing purposes, horizontal balers are treated as heavy manufacturing equipment — lenders typically require 2 years in business and full financial documentation. Vertical balers can often be financed with lighter documentation through equipment-specific lenders.

How much does a single-stream MRF sorting system cost?

A complete single-stream materials recovery facility (MRF) sorting system from CP Group, Machinex, or Bulk Handling Systems ranges from $1M to $10M+ depending on throughput capacity (typically measured in tons per hour). A small 10–15 TPH single-stream MRF starts around $1.5M–$3M for equipment only. Mid-range 30 TPH systems run $3M–$7M. These are large-scale capital projects typically financed through SBA 504 loans (for owner-operators), municipal bonds, or equipment-specific credit facilities arranged by the equipment manufacturers' financial divisions.

Can I finance a shredder for scrap metal processing?

Yes. Industrial shredders for scrap metal processing range from $15,000 (small single-shaft shredder) to $500,000+ (large hammermill shredder systems). Shredder financing is available through manufacturing equipment lenders who understand the scrap metal industry. Key factors lenders evaluate: years in business, scrap volume processed, commodity purchase contracts, and environmental compliance certifications. Established scrap processors with 2+ years of history and clean environmental records generally qualify for competitive terms at 48–72 months.

What e-waste processing equipment is available and how does it finance?

E-waste (electronic waste) processing equipment includes shredders ($25,000–$150,000), eddy current separators ($15,000–$80,000), air classifiers ($20,000–$100,000), and complete system lines ($100,000–$500,000+). E-waste processors require R2 or e-Stewards certification to operate commercially — lenders will ask for this certification as part of the application. E-waste equipment has good collateral value because it processes high-value metals (gold, silver, copper, palladium from circuit boards), giving lenders confidence in both cash flow and collateral.

Does recycling equipment qualify for Section 179 or bonus depreciation?

Yes. Recycling and waste processing equipment is tangible personal property that qualifies for Section 179 expensing (up to $2.5M in 2025) and 100% bonus depreciation (made permanent by the 2025 tax law) in the year placed in service. This is especially valuable for profitable recycling operations making major equipment purchases — a $200,000 horizontal baler can be fully expensed in year one, creating a significant tax reduction. Equipment financed through a loan or lease may still qualify for these deductions depending on the financing structure. Consult your tax advisor on specifics.
